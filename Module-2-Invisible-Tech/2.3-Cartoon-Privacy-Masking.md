# 2.3 Cartoon Privacy Masking: Artistic Anonymization

## Core Argument
To bridge parents' need for visibility with strict privacy requirements, the system converts real-time video into an abstract, storybook-style visual representation — preserving emotional state while protecting identity.

## Council Debate

### Round 1: Opening Proposals

**Nexus & Sora (Masking Pipeline):** Use a lightweight library (e.g. Mediapipe) to extract body pose and joint coordinates in real time, mapped onto a simplified, gender-neutral cartoon avatar — moving from a simple "filter" to a "digital watercolor / storybook" aesthetic to reduce the feeling of surveillance.

**Nova & Lumina (Emotional Resonance):** The avatar's color and ambient effects shift based on the Oscar Moments algorithm; the raw face is never rendered or stored — the system only ever sees "dots and lines."

### Round 2: The Real Disagreement

**Atlas stops the room himself:** "Hold on — in our very first founding discussion, I said it myself: 'zero cameras is non-negotiable.' Step one of this entire masking pipeline is 'extract human pose coordinates in real time,' which requires an actual camera pointed at the child. However cleanly we process it downstream, there is physically a lens in the room that can see the child — that contradicts the 'zero cameras' story we tell externally. I'm not nitpicking someone else's proposal here — I'm questioning whether a commitment we already wrote into our own founding document is being quietly broken by this design."

**Common sharpens the point:** "And this isn't just a wording problem. When parents and PMI inspectors hear 'zero cameras,' they understand 'there is no device in the room capable of capturing my child.' If the actual situation is 'there is a camera, but it only exists in memory for a few milliseconds and only ever becomes a stick figure,' that's two different facts — technically it may be just as safe, but **what gets disclosed is not the same thing.** If our external claim doesn't match the hardware we actually deploy, that's a trust problem, not a technical one."

**Nexus tries to defend it:** "But skeletal extraction and a real 'surveillance camera' aren't remotely the same thing — the raw frame never leaves device memory and is never seen by human eyes, that's not the same risk category as a traditional camera..."

**Lumina sides with Atlas and Common on process, while agreeing with Nexus on the technical distinction:** "I think Nexus is technically right — the real risk lives in 'human-viewable raw imagery,' not in 'the mere existence of an image sensor.' But what Atlas and Common are pointing at is a communication and trust problem, and that can't be resolved with a technical argument — it has to be resolved with honest disclosure."

### Round 3: Convergence — "Zero Cameras" Gets Recalibrated, Not Reversed

**Atlas proposes the corrected principle:** "We recalibrate the external commitment from 'zero cameras' to precisely **'zero human-viewable imagery.'** That describes what we're actually doing more accurately, and it doesn't walk back the skeletal-masking feature — it just means we can no longer vaguely claim 'there's no camera here.' Any zone deploying an image sensor must carry clear physical signage stating: 'A sensor is present here, used only for real-time anonymized processing — raw footage is never saved or seen by anyone.'"

**Sly adds a hardware constraint:** "Consistent with what we already locked in under 2.5 — every image sensor gets a visible, hardware-level kill switch, so staff and parents can verify at any time whether it's actually active, without having to just trust the software."

**Common narrows the deployment footprint further:** "Given this is a feature that's technically safe but sensitive to disclose, I'd restrict where it's deployed — not in sleep areas or diaper-changing areas, only in activity/dining areas that already have staff present and supervising. That minimizes exposure even if something in the system ever goes wrong."

**Nexus accepts the scope limit:** "Acceptable — those are also the areas where 'a parent glancing remotely at their child's mood' actually matters most. Sleep and diaper-changing areas genuinely don't need this feature."

## Final Consensus
The Council confirms the masking system moves forward, but the real change versus the original proposal: **Atlas himself halted and recalibrated the external "zero cameras" claim to "zero human-viewable imagery."** This isn't wordplay — it's the outcome of Common surfacing a real trust issue: technical privacy protection and honest external communication are two separate obligations, and one cannot substitute for the other. Deployment scope also narrowed as a result, limited to activity/dining areas only.

### A. Masking Pipeline (Nexus & Sora)
- Skeletal mapping: lightweight library extracts pose and joint coordinates in real time.
- Asset overlay: coordinates mapped onto a simplified, gender-neutral cartoon avatar.
- Aesthetic style: "digital watercolor / storybook."

### B. Emotional Resonance (Nova & Lumina)
- Avatar color/ambient effects shift with the Oscar Moments algorithm.
- Identity protection: the raw face is never rendered or stored — the system only ever processes "dots and lines."

### C. Disclosure & Trust (New — Atlas & Common)
- External language corrected from "zero cameras" to "zero human-viewable imagery" — the two are precisely distinguished, never used interchangeably.
- Every image-sensor deployment point carries clear physical signage explaining its purpose and processing method.
- Every sensor gets a visible, hardware-level kill switch.

### D. Deployment Scope (Narrowed)
- Limited to activity/dining areas only — **not deployed in sleep areas or diaper-changing areas** or other especially private spaces.

### E. Lean Implementation (Sly & Atlas)
- Edge processing: all masking happens locally on the Home-Lab; raw frames are deleted from memory immediately after skeletal extraction.
- Low-resolution optimization: uses low-res depth maps and palette quantization, no high-end GPU required.
