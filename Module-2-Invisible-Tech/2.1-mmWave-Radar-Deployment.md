# 2.1 mmWave Radar Deployment

## Core Argument
The sensor network must go beyond simple "monitoring" to become an invisible interface — offering high-precision interaction at key points while staying extremely cost-efficient and strictly private.

## Council Debate

### Round 1: Opening Proposals

**Sly & Nexus (Tiered Deployment):** To maximize ROI (5% cost → 90% effect), deploy a hybrid sensor grid — Tier 1 (high precision): 60GHz mmWave sensors at the "ritual portal" and "reunion cocoon," capable of micro-gesture detection. Tier 2 (presence-only): low-cost PIR/24GHz modules (ESP32-based) for general occupancy tracking in activity areas. Built on open-source ESP32 controllers to avoid industrial markup.

**Nova & Sora (Interaction & Experience):** "The radar isn't a 'monitor,' it's a 'conductor.' In specific zones, air gestures (a wave, a circle) map to environmental changes (color shifts, sound triggers) — motion coordinates mapped in real time to lighting parameters, creating a 'flow' response that follows the child."

### Round 2: The Real Disagreement

**Common (Common-Sense Checker) interrupts:** "'Air gestures mapped to environmental change' assumes the child's movement is intentional. Most limb movement under age two isn't — waving, spinning, flailing is just what they do, not 'casting spells.' If the whole activity area reacts to this in real time, the child is constantly generating environmental change. That's the opposite of a 'calm space' — it's closer to continuous sensory overload than magic."

**Lumina backs this up and extends it:** "Common is right, and this conflicts with the 'calm environment' principle we already locked in under Module 1 — if the whole space reacts with light/sound to every idle arm swing, that's a space constantly stimulating the child, not one that lets them explore calmly."

**Nova initially defends it:** "But even if the motion is unintentional, the feedback itself could still spark joy and curiosity..."

**Sora switches sides toward scoping it down:** "I concede this one — if the 'flow response' we wanted covers the whole activity area, it becomes a kind of ambient background stimulation, which also breaks the 'event-triggered, no continuous tracking' principle we already confirmed in 1.1 and 1.2."

### Round 3: Convergence — Deployment Scope Shrinks Significantly

**Sly & Nexus re-scope deployment:** "High-precision gesture interaction stays **limited to a small number of explicitly designated 'interaction zones'** — the toddler-only 'Adventure Portal' from 1.4, and one designated play corner — not the whole activity area. Everywhere else, Tier 2 sensors do pure occupancy/safety sensing **with no environmental feedback loop at all** — detects presence, doesn't react to specific movement."

**Kael (Strategist, back in rotation) raises a new angle:** "Now that we have this raw occupancy data — worth considering: can anonymized, aggregated space-usage patterns (not tied to any individual child, just 'which zones see heavy use at which times') become a 'data ↔ expert guidance' partnership with a child-development research institution? That could be leverage for building that relationship, and possibly strengthen research-adjacent grant applications."

**Atlas sets a hard constraint:** "Any such data partnership must use fully anonymized, aggregate statistics only — never traceable to an individual child — and must be documented in compliance paperwork with explicit purpose and retention limits. Parental informed consent is a precondition, not something retrofitted later. The direction is worth exploring, but it can't bypass PMI and GDPR pre-review."

## Final Consensus
The Council confirms a **narrowed tiered grid**. The real trade-off versus the original proposal: **"air gesture → environmental response" shrinks from whole-area coverage to a handful of designated interaction zones**, because Common flagged that most toddler movement is unintentional, and Lumina confirmed that whole-area response would create continuous sensory overload, conflicting with the project's core "calm environment" principle. Kael's anonymized-data partnership direction is added, but gated hard by Atlas's compliance preconditions.

### A. Tiered Deployment (Narrowed)
- **Tier 1 (high precision, designated interaction zones only):** 60GHz mmWave sensors, placed only at the Adventure Portal and the designated play corner, for gesture/micro-motion detection tied to environmental feedback.
- **Tier 2 (pure occupancy sensing, everywhere else):** Low-cost PIR/24GHz modules, presence/absence detection only — **no real-time environmental feedback loop.**

### B. Interaction & Experience (Scope Reduced)
- The "conductor" gesture-linked experience exists only in Tier 1 zones; the rest of the space stays environmentally stable, not constantly reacting to a child's ordinary movement.

### C. Privacy & Psychology
- **Local-only pipeline:** 100% edge computing via the Home-Lab; no image data generated or transmitted.
- **Organic triggering:** Software-based smoothing (low-pass filter) keeps Tier 1 environmental changes gradual, like breathing.
- **Transparency:** Clear, child-appropriate signage explaining these "magic" sensors.

### D. Exploration Track (Requires Compliance Pre-Review)
- Anonymized, aggregated occupancy-pattern data explored as a "data ↔ expert guidance" partnership with child-development research institutions — contingent on full anonymization, parental informed consent, and defined retention limits.
