# Parent-led Community Workshops

## Concept

Maison des Curieux is not only a micro-crèche. It is also a community where parents become contributors.

Each parent may propose an activity based on a personal passion or skill:
- woodworking
- cooking
- astronomy
- gardening
- robotics
- music
- sewing
- nature exploration
- etc.

Parents are both organizers and participants: they can host an activity and also enroll their own children in activities proposed by other families.

## Economic model

- Price: **10–15 € per child**, significantly below the usual market price.
- The organizing parent receives the workshop revenue.
- The micro-crèche provides the venue, scheduling, communication and safety framework.

No artificial limit is imposed on how often a parent may organize a workshop.

The community naturally regulates quality: workshops that interest families fill up; repetitive or less engaging workshops simply receive fewer registrations. Supply and demand are the same group of people — today's organizer is tomorrow's registrant — so the incentive to prepare a good workshop is self-reinforcing, without needing administrative caps or rules.

## Registration question

When a parent signs up to propose a workshop, they are not asked about their profession or qualifications. They are asked one question:

> **« Qu'avez-vous envie de transmettre ? »** — *What do you want to pass on?*

Not "what is your job," because a software engineer's real answer might be pizza-making, a doctor's might be birdwatching, a retiree's might be clock repair. Children meet real people, not professional labels — this is consistent with the project's broader philosophy of de-institutionalization.

## Curator role

Maison des Curieux is not a passive calendar of whatever parents happen to propose. It has a **curator** — not a teacher, not a boss — whose job is to notice gaps and invite balance:

- "There have already been four baking workshops this month — what about the parent who keeps bees?"
- "The kids have been obsessed with trains lately — is there a parent who works at SNCF?"
- "Autumn is here — time for a mushrooms/fallen-leaves/acorns workshop?"

This keeps the workshop library growing in variety rather than converging on whatever is easiest to propose.

## Le carnet des curieux

A small booklet each child keeps — not a stamp card, not a grading system. It records moments, not achievements:

> *I learned to make butter. I touched real wool. I smelled rosemary and thyme. I saw the craters of the moon. I built a kite. I met a real engineer. I learned to knit from a retired grandmother.*

No exams, no levels — just a trace of the people and the world a child encountered while growing up.

## Educational philosophy

Every adult has something worth transmitting.

Children discover not only professions, but passions, craftsmanship and everyday knowledge.

Parents are no longer only customers; they become active contributors to the learning community.

This strengthens social ties, values parental expertise and creates meaningful opportunities for parents to spend quality time with children while sharing what they genuinely love. Three effects matter most:

1. **It lowers the price barrier.** Many French children's ateliers cost 25–40€ a session, which strains families with two or more kids. Keeping it at 10–15€ makes it something a family can actually try on a weekend.
2. **It lets people discover their own knowledge has value.** A father who loves making pasta, a mother who paints in watercolor, someone who keeps bees, someone who studies mushrooms, someone who sews — most never think "I could run a workshop." Maison des Curieux becomes the platform that lets them.
3. **Children re-discover their parents.** Not just "mom is my mom," but "mom is also someone who bakes really good bread." Not just "dad is my dad," but "dad is also someone who's good with wood."

The real accumulating asset is not the space or the equipment — it is the community's own **skill map**, which grows richer over time and cannot be replicated by a large chain, because it belongs to this specific community.

## Guiding principle

> We do not build a marketplace of workshops.
>
> We cultivate a community where curiosity, generosity and knowledge circulate naturally.
